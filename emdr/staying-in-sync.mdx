---
title: "One movement, not three"
description: "Why the picture, the sound and the tap in your client's hand hold together — and what to do when the connection degrades"
---

In the room you set the tempo with your own hand, and it's right because
it's you. Remote, three things have to agree: what your client sees, what
they hear, and what they feel. If the tone lags the dot, you're no longer
doing bilateral stimulation — you're doing something else, and you can't
tell from your side of the screen.

That's the problem the EMDR Workspace is built around.

## The picture and the sound are the same value

Where the stimulus sits on its path, and which ear the tone is in, are the
same number. It's worked out once and used for both. There's no second
clock keeping the sound in step with the picture, because there's nothing
to keep in step — where the stimulus is **is** where the sound is.

So the tone can't slide away from the movement over a long set, and it
can't arrive a moment behind it. There's no drift to accumulate.

## A paired phone joins the same beat

If your client pairs a phone, it doesn't run its own rhythm alongside
yours. It joins the one already running, so the taps land on the turns of
the stimulus rather than near them.

Two phones — one in each hand — hold opposite halves of the same rhythm.
Neither phone knows about the other, and neither needs to: each works out
its own half from the same beat, so left and right stay exactly half a turn
apart for the whole set.

To pair one, open **Configure** and turn on **Client phone haptics**. A
card appears on your client's screen and they point their phone's camera at
it — nothing to install on Android, a small Rivet companion on iPhone.
**Add a second phone** issues a second code for the other hand. Your client
is never shown the code itself, only the card to scan.

## Changing speed mid-set

Change the speed without stopping. The picture, the sound and any paired
phone re-converge on the very next pass — you won't hear the tone wander
off the movement while the new pace settles, because there's nothing to
settle.

<Warning>
  Speed moves in small steps during a set and stops at **2 movements per
  second**. Published EMDR protocols sit below that, and faster flicker
  carries photosensitivity risk. The ceiling is there so you can move the
  control freely inside it without having to think about either. See
  [Photosensitive epilepsy safety](/emdr/photosensitive-epilepsy-safety).
</Warning>

## When the connection degrades

A weak connection degrades video here the way it does anywhere, and Rivet
won't stop a set on your behalf — a set runs its own timer regardless of
what the connection is doing.

So if the picture stalls or the sound breaks up mid-set, **stop the set
yourself** and get the connection back before you continue. A set you can't
observe isn't a set you're running.

## What reaches your client's side

The scene and the movement, and their own camera. Not your speed control,
not the readouts, not the prompts, not the ratings, and not the session
curve — none of that crosses over.

Nothing about the session is recorded, and nothing transcribes it.

## What sets don't do on their own

A set ends on its own timer and then waits for you. The next one doesn't
start until you start it. That's deliberate: the check-in between sets is
the work, and a timer isn't in a position to decide whether the next set
should run, run slower, or not run at all.
