Rivet doesn't replace your EHR. Jane, Owl, OWL Practice, your paper
chart — wherever you keep client records today — stays your system of
record. Rivet is the session layer: it holds the video call, the
structured templates you use during the session, the progress note you
write, and the client's record in Rivet. The handoff to your EHR is by
copy-paste or PDF attach.

The two systems work side by side. The rest of this page is how.

## How Rivet and your EHR fit together

Your EHR holds your full chart history — demographics, appointment
calendar, billing, insurance records, and the documentation continuity
that spans years of care. Rivet holds the session record: progress notes
(unsigned drafts and signed notes), completed assessments with scores,
and the client's activity timeline.

Rivet's job is to get a clean session note and the structured clinical
data from that session into your EHR. The export — a clipboard paste or
a PDF — is the handoff. Your EHR receives it and the chart continues
uninterrupted.

This is a deliberate framing: Rivet is where you work during the session;
your EHR is where the long-term record lives. The export keeps the two
current.

## The workflow, end to end

### Before the session

1. **Open the client's chart in your EHR** — Jane, Owl, paper — as you
   would for any other appointment. This is where the note will end
   up.
2. **Open the session in Rivet** — your client joins via your Rivet URL
   (`getrivet.ca/your-name`).

### During the session

3. **Pull templates as the session needs them** — PHQ-9 to start, a
   thought record midway, a target ID for EMDR, a safety plan if risk
   comes up. The structured data accumulates.
4. **Open your SOAP, DAP, or treatment plan** when you're ready to
   write — usually the last 5–10 minutes of the session, sometimes
   midway if you have time. The note auto-fills from the templates
   that completed earlier (see
   [Auto-fill from templates](/documentation/auto-fill-from-templates)).

### Ending the session

5. **Finish typing the note.** Dictate the parts you can, snip-rail the
   phrases you write every time, type the rest. Add private annotations
   where you want clinical reasoning preserved that the main field
   shouldn't carry. The video call can end while you're still typing —
   the note stays open.
6. **Export.** **Copy for Jane** for a clipboard paste, or
   Download as PDF for a file. Or run the full session-summary export
   to bundle every template with your note at the top. See
   [Exporting notes](/documentation/exporting-notes).

### After the session — into the EHR

7. **Paste into your EHR's session-note field.** Most practitioners use
   the clipboard export here — it pastes cleanly into Jane's note
   field, Owl's session field, or any free-text chart.
8. **Attach the PDF** if your EHR supports it and you want a
   visually-formatted archive copy alongside the text note.
9. **Close the overlay when you're done.** The note stays on the client's
   record in Rivet — your EHR and Rivet each hold a copy. Done.

## EHR-specific notes

### Jane / JaneApp

Jane's session-note field accepts plain text and preserves line breaks.
**Copy for Jane** → paste in. Section headings (Subjective:
/ Objective: / etc.) render as bold-ish heavier text in Jane's
viewer. Private annotations appear as italic indented lines below their
field.

If you use Jane's chart attachments, the session-summary PDF lands
clean as an attachment. Filename includes the date so it sorts
correctly.

### Owl / OWL Practice

Same as Jane — paste the clipboard export into the session-note field,
attach the PDF if you want a copy. Owl's text editor preserves line
breaks; the label/value format reads well.

### Paper chart

Practitioners with paper charts download the PDF, print it, and file
it. The PDF is designed for letter / A4 paper — generous margins,
sans-serif body, clear section breaks.

### Other EHRs

Anything that accepts text in a session-note field will accept the
clipboard export. Anything that accepts PDF attachments will accept
the per-template or session-summary PDF. If you have an EHR-specific
question, email **hello@getrivet.ca** and we'll work through it with
you.

## Per-client history in Rivet

Each client has a record in Rivet: a timeline of sessions, voicemails,
and messages; the completed assessments with scores and score-over-time
charts; and the progress notes from each session — unsigned drafts and
signed notes, labeled by status.

Before a session, you can open the client's record to review the PHQ-9
trend, read the prior note, or check what came up last time. During the
session, you work fresh templates and a new note. After the session, you
export into the EHR to keep the chart there current too.

The pattern that works: Rivet holds the session record; the EHR holds the
full chart. The export is the handoff that keeps them aligned.

## The line, restated

Rivet is the **session record** — notes, assessments, and the client
timeline. Your EHR is the **system of record** — the full chart, billing,
and long-term continuity. The export is the handoff that keeps both
current. Both tools are doing what they're best at, and the seam between
them is narrow enough to stay out of your way.
